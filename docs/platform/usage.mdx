description: "What your account holds and runs against its limits: the storage your workspace and conversations share, your workspace's files and checkouts, your conversations, your archive, each project's history, and your agents running at once."
---

**Settings → Usage** shows what your account holds and runs against its limits, with the share of each limit in use. It updates by itself while it is open. A limit comes from your plan, from an administrator, or from Octomerge's defaults, and the page says which. The Standard plan and a team's [Team plan](/platform/teams#the-team-plan) give the defaults below; the Pro plan gives more.

## Storage

On a plan, your storage holds your [workspace](#workspace) and your [conversations](#conversations) together: 10 GiB on the Standard plan and for each member of a team's Team plan, 50 GiB on the Pro plan. An administrator may set another limit for your account. **Storage** shows what each of them holds of it, and where the limit comes from.

From 80% of your storage the page warns you. Once your workspace and your conversations fill it:

- Agents take no new work: new messages, new sessions, sub-agents and scheduled sessions are refused, saying what holds your storage and what frees it. A response that is being written when your storage fills up finishes, and so does work already under way, such as a sub-agent finishing its task.
- Your workspace can't grow: a worker with new files stops for review, and new branches can't be created, as when your workspace is full.
- Reading, deleting and archiving keep working, and nothing is deleted for you.

To free space, delete conversations you no longer need (the largest are listed under [Conversations](#conversations)), archive or delete branches you no longer need, or remove files from your workspace. What you delete leaves your storage at once, and agents run again as soon as it is under its limit.

Where neither a plan nor an administrator sets your limit, your workspace holds it alone, and your conversations count toward no limit.

## Workspace

Your workspace is the files your workers synchronize: every branch's checkout, and your account's own files, such as your instructions, skills, plans and memory. Files a checkout ignores (such as `node_modules/` and build output) never leave their worker and don't count, and neither do archived branches.

| Limit | Default | Pro plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Files: regular files and symbolic links | 200,000 | 1,000,000 |
| Storage: the size of those files | 10 GiB | 50 GiB |
| Checkouts: one per branch | 5,000 | 5,000 |

On a plan, the size of your files counts toward the [storage](#storage) your workspace shares with your conversations, and the page shows it there. Storage counts a file once for each place it is in: two branches that hold the same file count it twice. Sizes are binary (1 GiB is 1,024 MiB).

From 80% of a limit the page warns you. Only growth past a limit is refused: once your workspace is full, a worker with new files stops for review (a workspace remediation chat explains what it holds back, and the worker publishes by itself once there is room again), and new branches can't be created, until the workspace is under its limit again. Editing and deleting files, and archiving, keep working.

### What uses your workspace

Under **What uses your workspace**, each project lists what its branches hold together, largest first, and your account's own files follow. Open a project to see each of its branches, with **Primary**, **Merged** (into the primary branch) and **Idle** (no conversation for 30 days) next to their names. A branch's **⋯** menu opens its files, or archives it: [archiving](/platform/archiving) takes the branch off your workers and out of your workspace limits, and keeps it on Octomerge until you unarchive it. The primary branch is archived only with its project.

`otti workspace status` shows the same totals against your limits.

## Conversations

**Conversations** shows what your conversations hold: their messages, tool calls and results, and the images and files stored for their models, at their full size, and the share of each. On a plan, they count toward the [storage](#storage) they share with your workspace. A conversation counts in full whatever its state: one you archived, and one whose history waits in [slower storage](/platform/persistence) because nobody opened it for a while. A sub-agent is a conversation of its own, and so is a fork, which holds its own copy of the history it was forked from. Records Octomerge keeps of each run and tool operation, and the [search index](/cli/otti#search-conversations), don't count. What a conversation adds counts within seconds.

Your largest conversations are listed below the total, largest first, with where each one is and when it was last active. Open one from its name, or delete it from its **⋯** menu: deleting a conversation erases its messages, tool results, images and files within minutes, and its size leaves the total at once. Its sub-agents stay, and so do files its agent saved on your workers ([Delete session…](/platform/workspace-chats)).

`otti workspace status` shows the same total.

## Archive

Archived branches count toward your archive quota instead of your workspace: 50 GiB of archived files (by size), 250 GiB on the Pro plan. Archiving more than the quota holds is refused; to make room, delete archived branches you no longer need, in **Archived** (under **More** in the sidebar) or with `otti -p PROJECT delete branch BRANCH --confirm`.

## Project history

Each project's history, its commits with every version of their files, may hold up to 1 GiB, 2 GiB on the Pro plan. We recommend keeping a project's history small: a smaller history is faster for your workers to fetch and for the Git page to show.

Only growth past the limit is refused. Importing a repository whose history is larger fails with its size and the limit. Once a project's history is full, new commits stay on the worker that made them, and `otti workspace status` says why; the project's history stays as it is, and its branches can still be created and deleted. [Contact us](https://octomerge.dev/contact) if a project needs more space.

A few habits keep a history small:

- Keep dependencies out of Git: a package manager, such as npm, Bundler or Maven, fetches them.
- Keep large binary files out of Git, or store them with Git LFS, so that the history holds only small pointers to them.
- Git is not a backup tool: back up with a tool made for it.

## Agents at once

Your account runs a limited number of agents at the same time: 10 by default, 25 on the Pro plan. When your limit is reached, new prompts, wakes and sub-agents wait in a queue and start by themselves as your runs end; the page shows how many wait. An Octomerge server also limits how many agents run at once for all of its accounts together ([Agents running at once](/get-started/add-worker#agents-running-at-once)).
