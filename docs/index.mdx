Octomerge brings project files, conversations, and agent tools into one workspace. Sign in with Google, Apple or GitHub, configure your own model provider, and connect otti-worker on a machine or VPS you control; on octomerge.dev, agents run with a [plan](#signing-up). New accounts start with no workers. Choose your worker and a project, then ask the agent for a useful change.

### Signing in

On octomerge.dev you sign in with Google, Apple or GitHub, through Octomerge's sign-in service, auth.octomerge.dev, and you can add the others to your account later in **Settings → Sign-in methods**. Signing in with GitHub gives Octomerge no access to your repositories: that is a separate grant in **Settings → Git**. auth.octomerge.dev keeps you signed in for seven days, so other apps that sign in with it don't ask again in that browser. An installation with other ways to sign in lists them on its first page, and **Sign in** then asks which one. See [Sign in](/get-started/sign-in).

**Log out** (in the sidebar's **More** tab and in **Settings → Sign-in sessions**) also ends that sign-in in the browser. **Sign out all devices** does the same and also ends your Octomerge sessions in every other browser. Neither touches your `otti` credentials or API keys, nor your sign-in at Google, Apple or GitHub itself.

### Signing up

The first time you sign in, Octomerge asks you to choose your **handle**, how people find you on Octomerge: in team invitations and on team pages. It's suggested from your GitHub username or your email (from your name when Apple hides your email), and you'll be able to change it later. On octomerge.dev it also asks how you heard about Octomerge; answering is optional. Your name and picture follow the way you signed up each time you sign in with it (signing in with another method you added leaves them alone); your email stays the one you signed up with. Then Octomerge opens [Set up your workspace](#set-up-your-workspace). If you came from **Start 7-day free trial** on [octomerge.dev](https://octomerge.dev/#pricing), it opens the plans first, with **Standard** chosen: start your trial there, and the setup follows its checkout. If you leave the checkout instead, **Set up your workspace** on the plans page takes you to the setup.

On octomerge.dev, agents run with a plan. Without one you can still set up your models, workers, browsers and settings, but agents don't run. Choose a plan in the checklist's **Plan** step, on [the plans page](https://octomerge.dev/plans) or in **Settings → Billing**; the plans and their prices are on [octomerge.dev](https://octomerge.dev/#pricing). **Standard** starts with 7 days free: you give your card at checkout, which Polar runs as Octomerge's merchant of record, and nothing is charged during the trial. Cancel in **Settings → Billing** before it ends and you pay nothing. There's one free trial per person. If a team with the [Team plan](/platform/teams#the-team-plan) invites you, accepting its invitation on the **Teams** page covers you instead, without a plan of your own.

Octomerge takes a limited number of paying accounts at a time (seats), and an account in its free trial counts as one. When all seats are taken, the plans say so and offer **Join the waitlist**, which gives you a place in line. When a seat opens, Octomerge emails you and holds it for you for 72 hours: choose a plan by then to take it; otherwise it goes to the next person in line, and you go to the back. Meanwhile your account works for everything but running agents. An invitation from a team whose Team plan runs doesn't wait for a seat: accepting it covers you at once.

### Set up your workspace

After you sign in, Octomerge opens **Set up your workspace**: a checklist with one step open at a time. **Models** adds a model connection, as **Settings → Models** does: [Open LLM Router](/platform/open-llm-router), where your installation offers it, or an API key. **Worker host** sets up where agents run, in numbered steps. The first is **Where will your agents run?**: a machine you have, such as your laptop, a home server or a VPS; or, where your installation offers them, a new [cloud server](/get-started/cloud-server) from a template, a [container](/get-started/worker-container) or a [boat.dev sandbox](/guides/boat-dev-worker). The steps after it follow your choice. For a machine you have: install the CLIs, sign the machine in (its code appears in the page, where you approve it) and start the worker. For a cloud server, container or sandbox: create a one-time enrollment token for the worker's label, then start the worker from the file Octomerge writes around it in the page; the token stays only in that page, so a reload forgets it and you can make a new one. A container usually can't run Docker, so agents on it can't build or run containers. Either way, the last step, **Connect the worker**, shows the worker when it connects, with the git, Node.js and Bun its agents' commands find and whether they can use Docker; for a cloud server, container or sandbox it also shows whether its token was used. Once it is connected, **Add another worker** starts the steps again for the next machine. **Access** (optional) gives every agent the same access in place: the GitHub repository grant (where your installation offers GitHub), account SSH keys for git hosts, [deploy targets](/platform/deploy-targets) (a sign-in to Vercel or Cloudflare returns to this step), secrets and variables, and your account `AGENTS.md`. **Browser** (recommended) opens with six short films of what agents do with your own browser: a tab you take over from your phone, every worker's `localhost` in one browser, the sites you're signed in to, sites that treat agents like you rather than like a bot, debugging with what DevTools sees, and busywork you hand off. They play one after another (with reduced motion, once you start them); choose one with its tab, or pause them. Below them are the commands for otti-browser on the computer you use every day, and skipping the step asks first. On octomerge.dev, **Plan** shows the plans ([Signing up](#signing-up)): choose one, or **Choose later**; your first task waits for a plan. **Ready** sums up what you set up and starts your first task with the composer of **New chat** (attach files, and choose the model, effort, worker and browser), in one of your projects, a GitHub repository (where your installation offers GitHub), an SSH remote or a new project without a remote. Sending queues the task as **New chat** does, with one step more for a new project: Octomerge creates the project (or imports the repository), then the chat, and shows each step under the composer. Starting without a browser asks once more (unless you skipped **Browser**), and the chat opens once the task is sent. Its **Follow from anywhere** shows a QR code that opens Octomerge on your phone and the `otti` commands to follow and steer a task from a terminal.

Without an account `AGENTS.md`, Access drafts one from your setup: your workers and which of them can build and run containers, your models with their efforts, the browser, git access, the variables deploy targets add and the ones you added, by name and never by value, and three working agreements you can change. **What this AGENTS.md covers** lists each topic as included, connected but not in the file (**Add it** adds it), or not included. **Fresh draft** writes the editor's text again from your setup, **Preview** shows it as **Settings → Instructions** does and **Markdown** edits it as text. Nothing is written until you choose **Save**, which never overwrites a change made meanwhile in another tab or by an agent; an existing `AGENTS.md` is shown as it is.

A step is done when what it sets up exists, so steps you have already set up show as done, and the checklist resumes where you left it. It opens after sign-in while you have no model connection, worker or chat (or, on octomerge.dev, no plan), until you choose **Close setup**. Reopen it any time from **More → Set up your workspace** in the sidebar.

Open **Settings** for your model connections, account instructions, secrets, MCP servers, workers, browser, notifications, privacy, Git and CLI access. Its first page shows each area's state, such as your default model or how many workers are ready. On octomerge.dev, **Settings → Privacy** turns **Help improve Octomerge** on or off: the records of your account's setup steps and use that show Octomerge where people get stuck, described in the [Privacy Policy](https://octomerge.dev/privacy). Turning it off deletes them. Production and stage have separate sessions, workspaces, storage, and credentials.

### AI connections

**Settings → Models** lists all your AI connections, each with its type (Anthropic, OpenAI, AWS Bedrock, OpenAI-compatible, or [Open LLM Router](/platform/open-llm-router)) and its models. Turn on the switch beside a connection to **enable** it: enabled connections are the model providers your chats and agents can choose. One of them is the **default**, marked and listed first, with its **Default model** below it: a chat runs on that model until you choose another, and so does a session an agent or `otti` starts without naming one. Choose another connection in the list (or **Make default** in its **⋯** menu), then confirm **Make default** to change the account default; it runs on its first model until you choose its default model. The default is always enabled, and the previous default stays enabled. **Add connection** asks for the type, then its name and credentials, then its models. A connection you add is enabled, and one you add while none is the default, such as your first, becomes the default. If you delete the default, another enabled connection becomes the default; without one, chats have no model until you add one, and the composer links to **Settings → Models**. A project or branch can have a different default connection and default model, chosen in its own settings.

### Models and efforts

A connection offers models by their IDs, such as `claude-opus-4-6`. A model can accept reasoning **efforts**, how much it thinks before it answers: **None**, **Minimal**, **Low**, **Medium**, **High**, **XHigh** and **Max**, as far as the provider takes them (Anthropic and AWS Bedrock Low to Max, OpenAI None to XHigh, an OpenAI-compatible endpoint any of them). Each message runs on one model at one of its efforts, and Octomerge sends that effort with every request. A model without efforts, such as an ID that pins one (`claude-opus-5-5-max`), takes none.

In a connection's editor each model has its **Model ID**, an optional display name and description, the **efforts it accepts** and its **Default effort**, its context window and an optional output limit; **Advanced: provider options** holds the provider's own options as JSON. Write the description for agents: say what the model is for, for example "Long investigations and design work". Agents read it in `otti get models`, and the model pickers show it. Descriptions are optional and at most 500 characters. New Anthropic, OpenAI and AWS Bedrock connections start with a few models you can change.

For an OpenAI-compatible connection, **Load models** lists the models its endpoint offers (`/models` under its base URL, read with its headers), by the names the endpoint gives them, with the efforts, context window and output limit it lists for each. Choose the ones to add: a model's listed name becomes its display name, and when the endpoint lists a model's efforts, they are filled in with its default effort; otherwise tick them after adding. A listed context window and output limit become the model's; without them it gets a context window of 200,000 tokens and the default output limit, 32,768 tokens. An endpoint lists them on each model as `name`, `efforts` (such as `["low", "medium", "high"]`), `default_effort`, `context_window` and `max_output_tokens` (whole numbers of tokens, such as `1000000` and `128000`); a model ID that pins an effort, such as `claude-opus-5-5-max`, can list it as `effort` and takes none.

A model's **Default effort** is the effort it runs at when a chat, an agent, a subagent or a schedule names the model without an effort, and the one your default model runs at. **Provider's default** keeps the one its provider gives it, shown with its value: what the endpoint lists, what a new connection starts with, or High (else Medium, else its highest effort) for efforts you tick yourself. Choose one of the model's efforts instead to run it at that one, for example `claude-opus-5-5` at **Max**; choose **Provider's default** again to go back. Your choice applies from the next chat, message or agent that names the model without an effort; chats and schedules keep the effort they already run at. An [Open LLM Router](/platform/open-llm-router#default-efforts) connection's models have the same choice under **Open LLM Router…** in its menu. `otti get models` and the composer's effort menu mark each model's default effort.

You choose a model and effort for each message in the composer, and for a launch link or a project's launch defaults. A connection saved before models had efforts used five tiers; when your installation updated, models that differed only by effort became one model with those efforts, each tier's description became its model's, and the model of the **Max** tier became the first. If a connection's models can't be read, it shows **Models need saving**: open its editor and add its models again. Octomerge doesn't run models of its own: a new conversation is named after the start of its first message until its agent names it ([chat names](/platform/workspace-chats#chat-names)).

### Choosing a model and effort in a chat

The model button at the bottom of the composer shows the model and effort your next message runs on, for example **Claude Opus 4.6 · High**. It opens the models of your enabled connections, grouped by connection, each with the efforts it takes and its description; your default model is marked **default**. Coding agents you run on your own computer, such as Claude Code and Codex, are listed under **Your agents** ([Bring your own agents](/platform/your-agents)). Below the list, choose the effort; the model's default effort has a dot, and a model without efforts says so. Switching models keeps the effort when the new model takes it, and otherwise uses the new model's default. Your choice applies from the next message you send, and the agent keeps working on it until another message changes it.

A model shows its maker's mark next to its name when its display name, its ID or its connection's name says who makes it, with one of these words:

- Claude's mark, for Anthropic: claude, opus, sonnet or haiku
- OpenAI's: gpt, codex or o3
- Qwen's: qwen or qwq
- DeepSeek's: deepseek
- Kimi's, for Moonshot AI: kimi or moonshot
- Z.ai's, for Zhipu's GLM: glm
- Mistral's: mistral, mixtral, codestral, devstral or magistral
- Meta's, for Llama: llama
- Gemini's, for Google: gemini or gemma

Every model of an Anthropic or OpenAI API key shows its company's mark. A model an endpoint serves under an ID of its own gets its mark from a display name that says it: the name its endpoint lists, or one you set in **Settings → Models**.

You can change the model, effort and worker while the agent is running. These are choices for your **next message**, not changes to work already in progress. Choose a worker and confirm **Use for next message**; the workspace moves only when that message is delivered. When the agent isn't running (it finished its turn, asked you something or was stopped), choosing another worker asks **Move this chat to …?**: sending your next message moves the chat there first, also when its current worker is offline or was removed, and the agent continues there ([Moving a chat to another worker](/platform/workspace-chats#moving-a-chat-to-another-worker)). **Queue** waits until the current turn finishes, while **Steer** uses the next model request after tool results. Each queued card shows its saved provider, model, effort and worker, and later composer changes do not change messages already queued. If the target worker disconnects or needs conflict resolution, the transfer waits rather than running on another worker. A move refused for a moment, because storage or the target worker is busy, is tried again for about a minute; if it still cannot go ahead, the chat stops with a notice saying what refused it and that nothing moved: **Resume** a minute later to try the move again.

What you send outside the composer chooses its model and worker too: your answers to the agent's questions, the environment values it asks for, dismissing either request, a message an MCP App view sends for you and **Resume** each show the model and worker chips, which start as the composer's choice and change only that one reply, message or resume.

A chat shows the model and effort of its last message. A new chat starts with the model and effort of the last message you sent from a composer, or with your default model at its default effort when you have not sent one yet or its connection is no longer enabled.

Agents choose models too. Ask, for example, "ask GPT to review the solution": the agent looks up your models with `otti get models`, each with its connection, efforts and description, and can start a subagent on any of them. A subagent it starts without naming a model runs on the conversation's own model and effort. `otti session start` and `session prompt` take `--model <id>` and `--effort <effort>` (see [otti](/cli/otti#models)).

### OpenAI-compatible connection headers

In **Settings → Models**, **Edit** in an OpenAI-compatible connection's **⋯** menu shows saved header names with hidden values. Untouched headers keep their saved values. Type a replacement to change just that header, or choose **Keep saved value** to undo a replacement. To rename a saved header, remove it and add the new name and value. **Clear all headers** explicitly removes the entire set when you save; **Reset header changes** discards header edits.

Choose **Reveal** beside a saved header to view its raw value. Only the signed-in connection owner can reveal values in the browser; CLI, API-key and agent credentials cannot. A reveal fetches only that header, does not mark it as edited, and is not included in normal settings responses. **Hide** masks it again; revealed values also hide after 30 seconds, when the tab becomes hidden, or when you close the editor. Typing into a revealed value is an explicit replacement.

Every agent request to an OpenAI-compatible connection also carries `X-Conversation-Id` (the chat's session ID) and `X-Conversation-Request-Id` (the agent step it belongs to), so a server or proxy you run can attribute token usage and prompt-cache statistics to a chat. Servers may ignore them; Anthropic, OpenAI and AWS Bedrock connections do not receive them.

Model tests use your unsaved header changes without storing them. **Save** applies edits without requiring a successful model request, so you can also remove authentication headers. New connections are validated before creation. **Clone** copies saved headers on the server without downloading their values unless you choose **Reveal**; edits in the clone form affect only the new connection.
